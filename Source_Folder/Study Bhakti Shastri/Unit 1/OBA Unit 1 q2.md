

Introduction 
Śrīmad Bhagavad-gītā is a divine conversation between the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, and Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukṣetra. It provides adetailed understanding of yoga, ultimately leading to bhakti-yoga, the highest goal. As stated in BG 1.1 (Purport): "It is the perfect theistic science spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead." 
The Bhagavad-gītā describes different yoga paths, all culminating in bhakti-yoga: 
- Karma-yoga (selfless action) – BG 3.9: "Work as a sacrifice for Lord."
- Jñāna-yoga (path of knowledge) – BG 4.10: "Understanding Kṛṣṇa leads to liberation." 
- Dhyāna-yoga (meditation) – BG 6.33: "Astanga yoga is very hard as ." 
- Bhakti-yoga (devotional service) – BG 6.47: "Kṛṣṇa declares the bhakti-yogī as the highest." 

Bhakti-yoga is superior to all other paths, as Kṛṣṇa explains to Arjuna. Other yoga systems are impractical in Kali-yuga, whereas bhakti-yoga includes the essential elements of all other yogas. Furthermore, in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Canto 11 (Uddhava Gītā), Kṛṣṇa confirms that bhakti is the highest form of worship.

The Impracticality of Other Yoga Systems in current age & time:
Karma-Yoga

Karma performed with selfish motives for sense gratification keeps one bound to the material world, as explained in BG 2.42-43, where Kṛṣṇa states that such activities entangle one in material desires. However, when one practices niṣkāma-karma-yoga—offering the results of work to the Lord and performing duties as a sacrifice—it leads to bhakti-yoga, as explained in BG 2.51 (Purport):

"Service for the cause of the Lord is called karma-yoga or buddhi-yoga, or in plain words, devotional service to the Lord."

Niskama Karma acts as an express elevator to bhakti-yoga** by quickly purifying the heart through selfless action. Ultimately, one must come to the platform of buddhi-yoga, which is essentially bhakti-yoga.

"So on the criterion of buddhi-yoga, on the criterion of devotional service to the Lord, you have to do everything. That is the, I mean to say, technique. That is the technique. Dūreṇa hy avaraṁ karma buddhi-yogāt." (Lecture on BG 2.49-51, New York, April 5, 1966)

Jñāna-Yoga

Understanding vast philosophical literature is extremely difficult and not accessible to all classes of men. One must be highly learned, and without the guidance of a bona fide Spiritual Master, grasping jñāna-yoga is nearly impossible. Though Śrīla Prabhupāda has mercifully translated Sanskrit scriptures into English and many other languages, those who cannot study deeply will find jñāna-yoga challenging. This path requires intense study and deep philosophical insight to differentiate between material and spiritual existence.

"As far as jñānīs are concerned, they are interested in jñāna-yoga, but even if one elevates oneself, after a great performance of austerity, to the Brahman effulgence, there is a chance of falling down again to the material world. Therefore jñāna-yoga does not actually end material existence." (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.25.29, Purport)

Dhyāna-Yoga

Kṛṣṇa clearly explains in BG 6.47 that one who worships Him with faith and devotion is the highest yogī. Śrīla Prabhupāda elaborates in the BG 6.47 (Purport):

"When one surpasses the aṣṭāṅga-yoga and comes to the point of the Supreme Personality of Godhead Kṛṣṇa, it is called bhakti-yoga, the culmination."

Chapter 6 describes how a yogī must follow the eight limbs of aṣṭāṅga-yoga, control the mind, sit in a secluded place, and adhere to numerous strict regulations. However, in this age of Kali, both the body and mind are weak, making aṣṭāṅga-yoga practically impossible.

"Similarly, regarding the haṭha-yoga system, which aims at the localized aspect of the Lord, Paramātmā, it has been experienced that many yogīs, such as Viśvāmitra, fall down. But bhakti-yogīs, once approaching the Supreme Personality of Godhead, never come back to this material world, as it is confirmed in the Bhagavad-gītā." (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.25.29, Purport)

In this age of Kali, bhakti-yoga is the only practical and effective path for spiritual realization. Karma-yoga can still entangle one in material bondage, jñāna-yoga requires deep philosophical study, and dhyāna-yoga is nearly impossible due to the restless nature of the mind. However, bhakti-yoga is accessible to all, regardless of qualification, as Kṛṣṇa confirms in BG 6.47:
"Of all yogīs, he who worships Me with faith and devotion is the highest."

Kṛṣṇa declares in BG 6.47:
